Dynamic Approval Workflow for Odoo

Draw the approval of any document instead of coding it.

Boxes are the states of the document, arrows are the buttons that move it. Click an arrow to say who can press it, how many approvals it needs, who is told and what else happens.

Most approval needs are the same: a document goes from draft to review to approved, different people can press different buttons, sometimes two people must agree, and the next approver has to hear about it. This module lets an administrator draw that process for any model, with no code, tells the right people at the right step (a pop-up, a to-do or an email), and shows the status and history on the form. It is an add-on to the flow you already have: it does not rewrite your model, so the original buttons, methods and states keep working. It can also be the approval flow of a model that has none.

What you get

Does not change your flow

Combine it with the workflow a model already has: an arrow can call the original button, so Odoo still does the real work, after the approval. Other modules and customisations are not rewritten.

Or a brand-new flow

Models without a status can have their own states, kept by the module, so you can build the whole approval process from scratch with no code, such as an eight-step business trip.

A diagram, not a form

Drag boxes, drag a dot from one box to another to add an arrow. Curved arrows bend around boxes, labels stay readable and boxes cannot overlap.

Who can press a button

Users, groups, a user taken from the document, or an expression. Add rules such as cost above 3000 to send a document to a different step.

Several approvals

One approval is enough, or one per approver line, or at least N. Waiting votes are reported (1/2) until the button goes through. Buttons can also be used from the list Action menu.

Tells the right people

Per arrow, tick who is told and how: a pop-up in Odoo, a To approve activity, or an email or inbox message. Approvers, the followers of the document and its creator can each be told.

A queue and reminders

Every employee gets Waiting for my approval, a list of the documents that wait for them. People who did not answer are reminded after the delay you choose, up to a limit.

Status and history

The form shows the flow's states and buttons, every move is posted in the chatter, and Approval history lists every vote and move. The Log and Notifications menus show who did and who was told what.

Test before you trust

The Test button shows, for a document and a user, which buttons would appear and why the others are hidden. A flow can be exported and imported as JSON.

How it works

Set up a flow once, then it works on the documents.

1. Create a flow

Open Approvals > Flows, choose a model and where the states come from. Add boxes and arrows in the designer.

2. Set the arrows

Click an arrow: choose the approvers, the number of approvals, rules, what happens when it is pressed, and who is told and how.

3. Use it

Users press the buttons on the form or from the list, and the next approver is told. Every vote and state change is recorded.

Step 1. Open your flows

Approvals > Flows lists the flows of your database. This demo has two: one on the purchase orders Odoo already has, and one for a business trip document that has no states of its own.

Step 2. The flow is a diagram

Eight steps and a Rejected state, drawn in a few minutes. Boxes are the states of the document, arrows are the buttons. The business trip has no state in Odoo, so the module keeps it.

Step 3. Click an arrow

Each arrow says who can press it, which rules must be true (here: a cost above 3000 goes to the director, a smaller one goes straight to booking), what happens when it is pressed and who is told.

Step 4. Choose who can press it, and who is told

Pick the approvers (users, a group, a user on the document, an expression) and how many approvals are needed. The table at the bottom says who is told and how: tick a cell to tell that person, or the followers of the document, in that way.

Step 5. Or reuse a button you already have

Make the arrow call the method of the original button. Type a few letters and the module suggests the public methods of the model, with the first line of their description. Odoo does the real work, only after the approval.

Step 6. A user starts a document

Alice, a traveler, creates a business trip. The status bar shows the steps of the flow and the only button she can press is Submit.

Step 7. Submit for approval

After Submit the trip waits in Manager review. A To approve activity is scheduled for her manager and the move is written in the chatter.

Step 8. The approver gets a pop-up

If the approver is online, a pop-up tells them at once that a document needs them, without waiting for an email.

Step 9. A queue of what is waiting

Waiting for my approval lists, for each person, the documents that wait for them, with the flow, the step and the time. People see only their own.

Step 10. Approve or reject

The manager opens the trip and sees Approve and Reject. Reject asks for a reason, and the people who cannot press these buttons see nothing.

Step 11. Each step shows only what the person can do

The trip costs more than 3000, so finance sees To director and not the direct approval. Rules on the arrows choose the route.

Step 12. The creator is told too

Every time somebody presses a button or votes, the person who created the document is told, with the reason if there is one.

Step 13. The whole history

Approval history, in the Action menu of the form, lists every vote and move: who, which button, from which step to which. Anybody who can read the document can open it.

Step 14. See who was told

Administrators can see who was told, in which way, and whether it was sent. A failing email never blocks an approval, it only shows up here.

Bonus. Several approvals for the same button

A large purchase order needs two approvals. After the first one the button shows (1/2) to the other approver, and the order moves only when the second one is given.

Requirements & Installation

The module depends on the standard base, web and mail modules and needs no external Python library.

The email and inbox notifications need a working outgoing mail server for real emails (an inbox message needs none). The activities and pop-ups need nothing more than the mail module.

Install the module

1. Copy the dwo_approval_flow folder into an add-ons path of your Odoo server.

2. Restart Odoo, activate developer mode and click Update Apps List in the Apps menu.

3. Search for Dynamic Approval Workflow and click Install.

Reload your browser page after installing so the new assets are loaded.

Create your first flow

Open the Approvals menu, then Flows, and click New. Choose the model and whether the states are the module's own or come from a field of the model.

Add states with + State, drag the dot of a box onto another box to add a button, then click the arrow to set approvers and actions.

Only users in the group Approval Flow / Administrator can edit flows. A flow can be exported and imported as JSON.

Click the Who can press it section of an arrow to choose who is told and how, and use Remind after (hours) on the flow to remind the people who did not answer.

What it does not do

A document follows one flow at a time. With several flows on a model, the first one whose Applies to filter matches is used. Two flows cannot approve the same document in parallel.

A button does not run by itself when a state is entered, and an arrow cannot loop back to its own box.

Nothing is sent for the first state of a new document, or when the document is moved by code that does not use an arrow. A button that anybody can press has no list of approvers, so it tells nobody. The text of a notification is in the language of the person who pressed the button.

The buttons of the form are the buttons of the flow: the module hides the original button where the flow calls its method, but code and RPC that call the method directly are not blocked.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can small orders go to one step and big ones to another?

Yes. Draw two arrows from the same box and give each a rule, for example amount below 5000 on one and amount of 5000 or more on the other. Only the arrow whose rule matches is shown.

Can a rejected document go back to draft?

Yes. A button can go to any box, so Reject can lead to Draft or to a Rejected state with a Reopen button.

Does it break the flow my model already has?

No. It adds to it. The original buttons and methods keep working: an arrow can call the original method (for example Confirm), so Odoo still does the work, only after the approval. If a model has exactly one flow, the original button that the flow calls is hidden in the states where the flow handles it, so users are not shown two ways to do the same thing.

Does it work with custom models?

Yes, any model that has a form view. Models with a state selection field can reuse it.

Can I build a brand-new approval process?

Yes. Choose a model, keep My own states and draw the states and buttons. The module keeps the state itself, so no change to the model is needed.

Who gets a notification, and can I choose how?

You choose per arrow. In the Who can press it dialog a table has one row per approver line and one for the followers of the document, and three columns: a pop-up in Odoo, a To approve activity and an email (or an inbox message, as each person prefers). The creator of the document can be told too. Nothing is sent until you tick a box.

What if an email cannot be sent?

The approval goes through anyway. Each try is written in the Notifications log with the error, and the activity and the pop-up still reach the person.

Do approvers who did not answer get a reminder?

Yes, if you set Remind after (hours) on the flow. They are reminded after that delay, up to the limit you set, and not any more once the document has moved on.
